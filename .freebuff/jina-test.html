People think I don't (really) use AI for Linkedin. So here's a non-edited screen recording of me 1) generating a viral topic 2) a caption 3) an image ☑ This strategy can be replicated in any industry. ☑ You are still the one controlling the input/output. ☑ You use AI as a tool - not as a one-hit solution. Absolutely no one complains about "AI-made". Because there is no such thing as "AI-made". There are GOOD posts and BAD posts. Here's my simple step-by-step process: ☑ Go to the new ChatGPT-5. ☑ Copy & paste the following prompt: "You are an expert at finding topics that go viral on Linkedin. Search the web deeply, and find 6x drastically different topics (with a title and a controversial twist attached) about "TOPIC". Your research must be tied to the news: we are in October, 2025." ☑ Select your favorite topic from the list. ☑ Ask for a follow-up prompt to ChatGPT. ☑ Prompt it to give more details/data about it. ☑ Once you're happy, copy the optimized topic. ☑ Paste it on app.easygen.io (the best for Linkedin) ☑ Generate at very least 3 to 7 different posts. ☑ Patchwork them into one mega post. ☑ Edit it. Add your own spice! Here you have it: 1. The right topic, selected for virality. 2. The right caption, generated & edited. That's an entire "AI-made" post from A to Z. But AI was not used to be lazier, but greater.

    I've grown tired of AI content and perfect, soulless LinkedIn posts. These days, most things you read are curated to a crisp, perfect grammar, catchy picture, hashtags, you name it. Everything is there. And yet, something is missing. Amidst all the typos, less-than-ideal phrasing choices and blunt sentences, a tiny little spark called the human spirit made every opinion unique and you could actually hear the voice of the person writing it. Let's take Balzac, the great French novelist. ChatGPT would blow Balzac out of the water - his writing style was sloppy, his vocabulary was poor and the plot itself wasn't too complex. But what would have been of Balzac's legacy if his texts would have been optimized by ChatGPT? And, as a psychologist, I ask myself another bigger question: What is happening to my brain, when I keep choosing the easy and "ultra correct" version of what I want to say? How am I exercising my brain and protecting myself from cognitive decline? Will I end up asking ChatGPT to provide a phrase to greet my grandchildren, because my own mind won't be able to muster something coherent? Now, don't get me wrong. Using AI definitely has a plethora of benefits and is good for many of our activities and productivity goals. I use it myself, on a daily basis even. But, as Marcus Aurelius once said: “The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way.” We are what we overcome. There is simply no shortcut to developing certain skills, critical thinking and a healthy brain in particular. We need the challenge, we need the stress, we need the mistakes, we need to get it wrong repeatedly so that we can learn, grow and stay sharp. "It's not just X — it's Y." "Quietly struggling with..." "🚨 Top 5 Reasons.." These have become like ads. Polished, historically proven to work and everywhere. In time, however, they have morphed into nothing more than irritating background noise that you actively try to ignore. We have enough of this already. The world needs more of YOU, my friend!
